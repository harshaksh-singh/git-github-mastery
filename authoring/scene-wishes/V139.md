| Badge | Without vigilant mode | With vigilant mode enabled by the person the commit names |
|---|---|---|
| "Verified" | "signed, and the signature verifies" | "signed and verified, and the committer is the only author who has enabled vigilant mode" |

## 4. CONCEPT, "**Persistent verification records.** Since the tenth" and "Two consequences, one good and one"

**Picture.** A timeline for one commit and one key.

1. The commit is verified; a record card is stamped and attached to the commit: `verified_at`.
2. The key's state changes along the timeline: "rotated", "revoked", "expired", "the contributor leaves the organization"; the record card does not change ("It can't be edited").
3. Consequence one (green): "Rotating a key no longer turns years of history "Unverified"."
4. Consequence two (amber): a stolen key signs a commit, is verified, is then revoked; the badge stays. Caption: "by date and by the audit log, not by the badge".

Date label for the feature: "Since the tenth of December 2024".

## 5. CONCEPT, the quiz, "B, GitHub's. Commits you make in", "**Why "Rebase and merge" yields unsigned**" and "**The require-signed-commits rule.** Display isn't enforcement."

**Picture.** Three merge methods as three lanes from a pull request to the base branch, each ending in a commit with its signature state, and then a gate "Require signed commits".

| Lane | What lands | Signature (documented) | At the gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Create a merge commit" | the merge commit | "signed by GitHub with its own key" | passes |
| "Squash and merge" | "the single commit" | "signed by GitHub with its own key" | passes |
| "Rebase and merge" | new commits "with new parents and a new committer" | "created without signature verification" | refused (red cross) |

Steps: the three lanes; quiz hold on the squash lane with three key choices "yours", "GitHub's", "nobody's"; GitHub's key lights up, with the note "on behalf of a signed-in user"; the rebase lane: the original signatures fall off as the commits are copied ("can't be carried over"), and a dashed key "your private key" stays outside GitHub; then the gate. The dashed card of picture 1 is filled here.

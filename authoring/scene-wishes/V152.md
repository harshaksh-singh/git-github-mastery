# Scene wishes for V152: Runners, limits and billing, and the investigation order for a failing workflow

Spots are given as section heading plus the first six words of the paragraph in `video/scripts/V152-*.md`. Every label is copied from the script. Limits, prices and dates are quoted from section 20B.10 and several are "announced": every dated label keeps its date and the word "announced" where the script has it. One library scene is already placed (`graph` in two states, LIVE TERMINAL DEMO, "`main` has a commit by Asha," and "The graph shows the merge `9c7cc7e`").

## 1. The life of a hosted runner

- **Spot.** CONCEPT, "**Hosted runners. In one sentence:** a".
- **Picture.** An image (a template) on the left, a machine in the middle, a bin on the right.
- **Steps.**
  1. `image`: "a published image".
  2. `fresh`: "a fresh virtual machine" is stamped out of the image.
  3. `one-job`: one job runs on it.
  4. `destroyed`: the machine is removed; a second job gets a second machine from the image.
- **Why not today.** `ci` shows a runner but not where it comes from or where it goes.

  1. `today`: `ubuntu-latest` on "Ubuntu 24.04", dated "1 October 2026".
  2. `announced`: a span "19 October to 19 November 2026", marked "announced"; the label slides gradually toward "Ubuntu 26.04".

  5. `retire`: a calendar mark; caption "you must move the label yourself before the image retires".
- **Why not today.** No scene for a label on something that is not a commit, and no timeline.

## 6. The investigation order as a ladder

- **Picture.** Twelve rungs, revealed one at a time, the word first and then its question.
- **Steps.** One per rung, with the labels of the DIAGRAM table: "workflow", "event", "permissions", "runner", "environment", "dependencies", "secrets", "action versions", then a horizontal rule "decided before the first log line", then "logs", "artifacts", "cache", "concurrency". ## 7. A chain of decisions, and the log at its end

- **Spot.** MENTAL MODEL, "**A model for the investigation order.**", then "The log is the output of".
- **Picture.** A chain of links, each a question: "Was a run created, from which file?", "For which commit?", "With what authority?", "On what machine?", "Past which gate?", "With which dependencies, which secrets, which action code?" The last link prints a log.
- **Steps.**
  1. `chain`: the links arrive in order; the log comes out of the last one.

  3. `faithful`: the log is unchanged; caption "a faithful record of the wrong experiment".
- **Why not today.** No scene of this kind.

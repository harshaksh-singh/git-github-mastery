## 5. A cache is a shared, unsigned directory

- **Spot.** CONCEPT, "**Caches and artifacts. In one sentence:** a cache" through "That's the third sentence of the hook, answered."; HOOK, the third quoted sentence ("Our release job has `contents: read`.").
- **Picture.** A shelf labelled "cache, default branch scope", entries keyed by a string. Two runs: a low-trust run on the left, the release job on the right with a badge `contents: read`.
- **Steps.** `write`: the low-trust run puts an entry on the shelf; the badge `contents: read` on the other job does not react, caption quoted from the post-mortem: "cache writes use a runner-internal token, not the workflow `GITHUB_TOKEN`". `restore`: the release job computes the same key and takes that entry, caption "cache contents are not signed or verified". `june`: date "26 June 2026"; the shelf gets a slot that only seven trigger chips may write: `push`, `workflow_dispatch`, `repository_dispatch`, `delete`, `registry_package`, `page_build`, `schedule`; every other trigger gets "read-only access". `september`: date "10 September 2026"; a dial `cache-mode` with the values `read`, `write`, `write-only`, `none`; on the job `publish` it points at `none`, caption "a job that holds a publishing identity restores nothing it did not build".
- Artifacts as a second shelf with the quoted label "untrusted data, since their contents can come from a fork".

### Imperfections that apply to several videos

- **A headline can outlive its topic.** A bold lead-in becomes the slide headline and stays until the next bold lead-in, so a quiz or a transition paragraph that an editor added after a topic is shown under the previous headline (seen in V004: "Prevention" over the layer quiz). The fix belongs in the scripts (a bold lead-in for the quiz), which was outside what this pass may edit.
- **A held scene keeps its last caption.** When a scene is held under paragraphs that talk about something else, its caption line still describes the last step ("add copies the working tree into the index" under two paragraphs about `git status` in V011). `say_<step>=` and `captions=off` now allow a script to avoid this; it was changed only where the caption contradicted the narration.
- **Quizzes asked over the table that answers them.** Three were found and fixed by letting the table leave first (V014, V017, V019) and one by ending a scene (V020). V027's precedence quiz is still asked over the scope table, which shows the reading order but not the answer.
- **Edges close to IDs.** In graphs with a diagonal edge, the edge can graze the last character of an ID under the commit it leaves (V031 criss-cross) or pass close to a tag chip (V024). V023 was redrawn to avoid the two bad cases.
- **Only commit graphs are re-framed.** The camera re-fit follows commit graphs (graph, merge). The object-model scene is still framed for both commits from the start (V008: empty lower half until the second commit).
- **Explainer share.** The brief aimed at a fifth of each video. Batch one is mostly transcript-driven (reading `.git`, status codes, configuration), so the share runs from 3 percent (V028, configuration transcripts) to 27 percent (V031); 12 of the 33 are at 18 percent or more. Nothing was forced.

## 5. Technical read of the editors' additions (V002 to V033)

Method: for every script, every narration sentence that does not occur in `video/scripts-original/` (48 to 96 sentences per script, about 2,050 in all) was listed and read against `reference/glossary.md` and the cited chapter. Seven definitions were imprecise and were corrected; the rest was accurate, and every number that looked new (dates, versions, vote counts, poll figures) was found in the original script or the textbook.

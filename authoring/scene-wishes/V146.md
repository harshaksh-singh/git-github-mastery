## 1. Jobs in order: `needs`

- **Spot.** CONCEPT, "`needs`, at job level: the job".
- **Picture.** Job boxes joined by arrows.
- **Steps.**
  1. `parallel`: two job boxes side by side with no arrow; caption "Without `needs`, jobs run in parallel."
  2. `needs`: an arrow labelled `needs` from the first to the second; the second waits.
  3. `failed`: the first job gets a red cross; the second turns grey with the word "skipped"; caption "If one of them fails or is skipped, this job is skipped".
  4. `unless`: an `if` chip lands on the second job and it runs; caption "unless its `if` says otherwise".
- **Why not today.** `ci` draws one job only.

## 3. Two rules about skipping

- **Spot.** CONCEPT, "B, and most people say A", then the two paragraphs "A job skipped by its `if`" and "A workflow that never starts, because".

## 5. Where a cache is looked for

- **Spot.** CONCEPT, "The restore order. First the exact", through "The `cache-hit` output: true only for".
- **Picture.** A shelf of labelled boxes (cache entries) and a key being compared with the labels.
- **Steps.**
  1. `exact`: "the exact key": one box matches whole; read-out `cache-hit` true.
  2. `starts-with`: "entries whose key starts with the key".
  3. `restore-keys`: "each `restore-keys` prefix in order, the most recently created match winning"; read-out `cache-hit` false.
  4. `miss`: nothing matches; caption "on a miss, the cache is saved at the end of the job, and only if the job completes successfully".
  5. `immutable`: a lock on a box; quotation "You cannot change the contents of an existing cache."
- **Why not today.** No scene for a keyed store.

## 6. Who can read which cache

- **Spot.** CONCEPT, "Scope: a run can restore caches".
- **Picture.** Branch lanes with a cache box on each.
- **Steps.**
  1. `own`: a run on a branch reads the cache "created on its own branch".
  2. `default`: it also reads "the default branch".
  3. `base`: a pull request run "can also restore from its base branch".
  4. `siblings`: an arrow between two sibling branches is refused with a cross: "Sibling branches can't read each other's caches."
  5. `merge-ref`: a box on "the merge ref", readable "only by re-runs of that pull request".
- **Why not today.** No scene for scopes.

## 7. Cache or artifact

- **Spot.** MENTAL MODEL, "Then the documentation's rule for choosing", through "So when you see data moving".
- **Picture.** Two cards side by side: the fridge box and a sealed parcel.
- **Steps.**

  2. `artifact`: card "artifact": "files a job produced that must be passed to another job or kept after the run, such as build outputs and test reports"; mark "the bytes you download are the bytes that were uploaded".

## 8. Build once, deploy what was tested

- **Spot.** MENTAL MODEL, "And the reason behind that rule,".
- **Picture.** Two pipelines, one above the other.
- **Steps.**
  1. `rebuild`: build job, test job, then a deploy job that builds again; the second build "resolves dependencies again, possibly to different versions"; the two outputs get an unequal sign.
  2. `artifact`: build job uploads an artifact; the deploy job downloads it; an equals sign; caption "the files that were tested are the files that are deployed".
- **Why not today.** `ci` draws one job and no file passing between jobs.

## 5. The boundary that `--onto` needs

- **When.** After the reveal.
- **Spot.** MENTAL MODEL, "Where the picture breaks: with paper"; again at LIVE TERMINAL DEMO, "**Step 8: restore the correct history.**".
- **Picture.** The old series as one row, with a bracket.
- **Steps.**
  1. `boundary`: a marker on `4967e71`: "the old shared tip". "Everything after it on your side is yours": `af1f6de`, `57b5972` are bracketed.
  2. `onto`: the command `git rebase --onto d4fd03c 4967e71` with an arrow from each argument to its commit: "new tip" and "old shared tip".
  3. `replay`: only the two bracketed commits are lifted and land on `d4fd03c` as `021ec35` and `5e4c03f`.
  4. `without`: a faded second run without the boundary, in which the three old copies are lifted too: "Git treats the old copies of the shared commits as yours and replays them too" (from the common mistakes).
- **Why not today.** `rebase` lifts a whole branch; it has no boundary argument.

## 1. A line that looks like a gate

- **Spot.** HOOK, "And here's how a team finds", through "The gate had never been configured,".
- **Picture.** The line `environment: production` with a gate drawn behind it in dashed outline.
- **Steps.**
  1. `assumed`: the line and a solid-looking gate; caption "Everyone has read that line and assumed a gate."
  2. `through`: a job token walks through the gate without stopping; caption "a deployment goes to production without anyone approving it".
  3. `empty`: the gate turns dashed and shows "rules: none"; caption "naming an environment that does not exist creates it, without any rule".
- **Why not today.** No scene for a gate.

## 2. The gate is in front of the machine

- **Spot.** CONCEPT, "First: all protection rules must pass".
- **Picture.** Left to right: a job, a door labelled with the environment name, a runner, and inside the runner a key cabinet (the environment's secrets).
- **Steps.**
  1. `waits`: the job stands in front of the closed door; the runner box is empty outline: "no runner".
  2. `rules`: locks on the door open one at a time: "Required reviewers", "Wait timer", "Deployment branches and tags".
  3. `runner`: the door opens; only now the runner appears and the job enters.
  4. `secrets`: the key cabinet opens; caption "secrets and variables the job can read only after that".
  5. `not-a-step`: a crossed-out alternative: the gate drawn as a step inside the job; caption "It is not a step inside the job that the job could skip."
